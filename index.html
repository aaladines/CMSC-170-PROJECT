Problem Statement and Motivation

Motivation

Many agricultural products cannot be stored for a long time such as vegetables and root crops due to their short shelf life. They spoil quickly and cannot be stored for a long time, which means that a drop in supply may lead to higher prices.

If the prediction of the production of vegetable crop in a province is expected to be lower than the same quarter of the previous year, the government, in connection with agricultural traders from other provinces that have higher production can supply it to the province that needs to more just to stabilize prices. In contrast, if the production were to increase, farmers and traders could supply it to other provinces that are in need.

Problem Statement

Can KNN, Gaussian Naive Bayes, Perceptron predict whether a province's quarterly production of a vegetable or root crop increases or decreases compared with the same quarter of the previous year using past data?

Dataset Description

Overview and Size

The dataset includes a variety of vegetable and root crop quarterly, semestral, and annual production data for every province in the Philippines from 2010-2026. After preprocessing, the dataset has 9920 rows.

Source

Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA)

Features and Target

To accurately forecast whether a province's quarterly yield for top vegetable and root crops (based on Philippine agricultural crop production data) will increase (Class 1) or decrease/remain stagnant (Class 0) compared to the exact same quarter of the previous calendar year.

Preprocessing Steps

  • The dataset was prepared by keeping the ten selected crops and regional production records, excluding the Negros Island Region (NIR), provincial records, and the national total.
  • Semester and annual totals were removed, leaving only quarterly observations grouped by crop and region.
  • Region names were cleaned by removing leading dots and extra spaces, and production values were converted into numeric form.
  • Missing values marked as ".." were skipped, while actual zero-production values were preserved.
  • Each quarter was paired with the same quarter of the previous year, and pairs with missing observations were excluded.
  • Production was labeled 1 if it increased and 0 if it decreased.

Methods

K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)

KNN is an instance-based learning algorithm that classifies a new data point by finding the k most similar historical samples in the dataset and taking a majority vote of their outcomes. This method is highly appropriate for our problem because agricultural patterns are often cyclical and localized. Provinces with similar recent production trends and seasonal positions are likely to experience similar future outcomes. Because KNN makes no underlying assumptions about the mathematical distribution of the data, it can effectively capture these localized, repeating patterns that a global model might overlook. By calculating the Euclidean distance between standardized features (such as previous year-over-year changes and log volumes), KNN will predict whether a province's quarterly production will increase or decrease based on what occurred during historically similar quarters.

Gaussian Naive Bayes

Gaussian Naive Bayes is a probabilistic, generative classifier based on Bayes' Theorem that estimates the mean and variance of each feature for both the "increase" and "decrease" classes. It is suitable for this problem because our dataset consists of continuous numerical features (such as production volume in metric tons), allowing the Gaussian variant to model these features without requiring us to discretize or categorize the continuous data. While the assumption of feature independence is likely violated in time-series agricultural data (since lagged production changes are correlated), using Gaussian Naive Bayes serves as an excellent probabilistic baseline that contrasts sharply with the other chosen methods.

Perceptron

The Perceptron is a linear classifier that iteratively learns a weight vector and bias to draw a separating hyperplane between the two classes. This algorithm is appropriate for our project because it directly tests whether agricultural production increases and decreases can be linearly separated within our feature space. It provides a simple, efficient linear baseline that heavily contrasts with both the instance-based approach of KNN and the probabilistic approach of Naive Bayes. By passing normalized input features through a step activation function and updating weights over several epochs, the Perceptron will attempt to learn a definitive boundary. If the agricultural data proves too noisy or not linearly separable, observing its failure to converge will provide valuable insights for our evaluation discussion.

Data Setup

Data Splitting (Training/Testing)

In training and splitting the data, the group used 80% for training and 20% for testing, applied separately within each year instead of once over the whole dataset. The rows of each year were assigned to the training or testing set using a random number generator, so that the testing data is not biased toward the later years or toward any one quarter, such as Q4. For the dataset, the total amount of available data was 9920 rows, hence the amount of available training and testing rows are 7936 and 1984, respectively. This gives 512 training and 128 testing rows for each year from 2011 to 2025, and 256 training and 64 testing rows for 2026.

Evaluation Metrics

To evaluate the performance of the three implemented machine learning algorithms, namely, Gaussian Naive Bayes (GNB), K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), and Perceptron, we used four evaluation metrics: Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and F1-score.

Variables

  • True Positive (TP): The actual label is 1 and the predicted label is also 1.
  • True Negative (TN): The actual label is 0 and the predicted label is also 0.
  • False Positive (FP): The label is 0, but the predicted label is 1.
  • False Negative (FN): The label is 1, but the predicted label is 0.

Accuracy measures the proportion of correctly predicted records out of all testing records. It provides an overall measure of how often the model makes correct predictions.
Accuracy = (TP + TN) / (TP + TN + FP + FN)

Precision measures the proportion of records predicted as an increase that actually increases. It indicates how reliable the model's positive predictions are.
Precision = TP / (TP + FP)

Recall measures the proportion of actual increases that are correctly identified by the model. It indicates how well the model detects increases in crop production.
Recall = TP / (TP + FN)

F1 score provides a combined measure of both Precision and Recall. This is useful for assessing the balance between identifying actual increases and avoiding incorrect increase predictions.
F1 score = (2 * Precision * Recall) / (Precision + Recall)

Findings

K-Nearest Neighbor emerged as the strongest overall algorithm under the 80/20 per-year split, achieving the highest 16-year average Accuracy (0.6196), precision (0.6417), and f1-score (0.6386), while ranking first or tied in accuracy in 10 out of 16 years and precision in 9 out of 16 years.

Perceptron achieved the second-highest 16 year average f1-score (0.6130), ahead of Gaussian Naive Bayes (0.6110), alongside 0.5801 average accuracy, 0.5997 average precision, and 0.6736 average recall.

Gaussian Naive Bayes recorded the highest 16 year average recall (0.6941), ranking first or tied in recall in 10 out of 16 years and f1-score in 8 out of 16 years.
